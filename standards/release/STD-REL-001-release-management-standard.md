# STD-REL-001 – Release Management Standard

## Purpose

Govern release planning, readiness, approval, execution, verification, support, and closure through attributable evidence and human decisions.

## Scope

Applies to planned, emergency, software, documentation, configuration, infrastructure, and compatible composite releases into governed environments.

## Definitions

- **Release manifest:** Versioned inventory of approved artifacts, source revisions, dependencies, changes, and target environments.
- **Go/no-go decision:** Recorded human authorization to proceed, defer, or cancel a release.
- **Hypercare:** Time-bounded enhanced observation and support after release.

## Principles

Releases are outcome-based, traceable, evidence-driven, compatible, recoverable, communicated, operationally supported, and approved by accountable humans.

## Applicability

Every governed release MUST have controls proportionate to business, security, compliance, operational, and change risk.

## Mandatory Rules

1. **Release governance and plan.** Each release MUST follow the approved release policy and define scope, exclusions, owner, classification, schedule aligned to the release calendar, dependencies, feature readiness, stakeholders, communication, support, risk assessment, and success criteria. Evidence: approved release plan and calendar entry.
2. **Version and identity.** Releases MUST use a documented version strategy and unique identifier, with traceability to source revisions, immutable artifacts, dependencies, and compatibility commitments. Evidence: release manifest.
3. **Readiness criteria.** Required architecture approval, security approval, functional and regression test completion, performance evidence, deployment readiness, documentation completion, operational readiness, support readiness, and risk assessment MUST be complete or explicitly dispositioned before go/no-go. Evidence: readiness record and approval references.
4. **Compatibility.** Breaking changes, migrations, deprecations, consumer impact, and support windows MUST have approved communication and transition plans. Evidence: compatibility assessment.
5. **Decision authority.** Go/no-go criteria, authorized approvers, separation of implementation and approval, and change-management integration MUST be defined. AI agents MUST NOT approve a release. Evidence: dated approval record.
6. **Exceptions.** Failed controls, accepted defects, unresolved findings, and readiness exceptions MUST identify scope, risk, owner, compensating controls, expiry, and authorized human disposition. Evidence: exception register.
7. **Execution coordination.** Deployment sequence, responsibilities, dependencies, communication plan, support handoff, verification activities, rollback readiness, release monitoring, and decision points MUST be coordinated against the approved manifest. Evidence: execution and change record.
8. **Release notes.** Release notes MUST describe changes, compatibility, migrations, deprecations, known issues, security considerations, support, and recovery without exposing sensitive data. Evidence: reviewed notes.
9. **Emergency release.** Emergency scope MUST be minimized and linked to authorized incident or change authority, minimum validation, recovery, stakeholder communication, and retrospective review. Evidence: emergency release record.
10. **Release validation.** Smoke testing, production verification, service health, security, performance, business validation, declared success criteria, and release acceptance MUST be evaluated after release by accountable owners. Evidence: production-verification and acceptance record.
11. **Hypercare and closure.** Material releases MUST define observation period, owners, escalation, defect disposition, release documentation, lessons learned, metrics collection, audit evidence, post-release review, and closure authority. Evidence: hypercare, post-release review, and closure record.
12. **Metrics.** Owners MUST review release frequency, change failure rate, mean time to recovery, release success rate, rollback rate, escaped defects, readiness exceptions, emergency releases, and hypercare incidents with defined calculation scopes and without hiding failures. Evidence: metric definitions and trend report.
13. **Final accountability.** Only authorized humans MAY accept residual release risk, approve go/no-go, and close the release. Evidence generated by automation or AI MUST remain independently reviewable.
14. **Operational controls.** Release execution MUST comply with change governance, segregation of duties, approved release-freeze periods, and applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and enterprise requirements. Emergency releases MUST retain designated human authorization, minimum safe validation, traceability, recovery, and retrospective review. Evidence: change record, freeze exception where applicable, compliance review, and emergency-release approval.

## Recommended Practices

- Teams SHOULD prefer predictable release cadences and smaller release scope when product constraints allow.
- Readiness SHOULD be assembled continuously rather than immediately before the go/no-go decision.
- Post-release reviews SHOULD focus on system improvement rather than individual blame.

## Prohibited Practices or Anti-Patterns

- Teams MUST NOT release untraceable artifacts, bypass blocking evidence, fabricate readiness, use emergency classification for convenience, or treat pipeline success as approval.
- Implementers and AI agents MUST NOT provide the sole required go/no-go decision.

## AI Implementation Guidance

AI agents MAY prepare release documentation, verify release checklists, summarize test evidence, generate release notes, assist with release risk analysis, analyze release metrics, and draft plans, manifests, communications, or post-release analyses. Agents MUST preserve source provenance, disclose gaps, and escalate failed controls.

AI agents MUST NOT approve production releases, authorize emergency releases, bypass release gates, waive mandatory approvals, override change management, declare production readiness, accept risk, change authoritative evidence, communicate externally without approval, or close releases.

## Human Review Guidance

The release manager coordinates evidence and execution; product and business owners confirm outcomes; QA, security, architecture, operations, support, and compliance reviewers confirm applicable readiness. The designated release authority makes go/no-go decisions, and the Product Owner approves this standard.

## Required Evidence

- Approved release plan, manifest, source and artifact traceability, dependency and compatibility assessment
- Test evidence, security, performance, documentation, deployment readiness, operational, support, and risk evidence
- Change record, deployment record, go/no-go and exception approval records, release notes, communication record, and rollback plan
- Production verification, hypercare observations, defects, metrics, post-release review, and closure decision

Evidence MUST be attributable, version-aligned, retained, reviewable, and linked to accountable owners and decisions.

## Validation Rules

- Automated checks SHOULD validate manifest completeness, artifact identity, required evidence, version rules, and blocking status where tooling exists.
- Human review MUST assess business readiness, exceptions, compatibility, support, recovery, residual risk, and approval authority.
- Automated completion MUST NOT mark a release approved or closed.

## Exceptions

Exceptions follow the [Standards Review Process](../../docs/governance/STANDARD_REVIEW_PROCESS.md) and require bounded scope, risk, owner, compensating controls, approver, expiry, monitoring, and remediation. Emergency sequencing requires retrospective human review.
